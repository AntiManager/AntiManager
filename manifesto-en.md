# Manifestum Imperii Rationalis

**The Management Systems Manifesto**

## Preamble

We are the engineers of human management systems, the architects of order, and the guarantors of justice. We are no longer "managers" in the old sense of the word. Our task is not to control people, but to design and launch social machines that unlock each individual's potential to achieve common goals. We find that through a conscious approach to crafting the environment, we achieve superior outcomes. Acknowledging the historical continuity of management thought, we proclaim this manifesto.

## 10 Values — A Choice for Evolution, Not Dogma

1. **People and their potential** over blind adherence to instructions.
   *We value initiative and intellect higher than mere rule-following, while recognizing that discipline and compliance are a necessary foundation for starting any endeavor.*

2. **A working and fair system** over heroism and firefighting.
   *We invest effort in building stable and predictable processes, rather than encouraging a regime of perpetual "heroic effort," though we are infinitely grateful to those who save the day in a crisis.*

3. **Collaboration and trust** over total control and suspicion.
   *We value openness and mutual support higher than attempts to control every step, though audit and reporting are essential tools for transparency, not distrust.*

4. **Continuous process improvement** over blame-seeking.
   *We view any error as a symptom of a systemic failure and an opportunity for improvement, not as a reason to punish an individual, though personal accountability and the duty to rectify consequences remain unwavering.*

5. **Meaning and intentionality** over blind trend-following.
   *We implement any innovation only when we understand what pain it solves and what meaning is embedded within it, while remaining open and curious about all things new.*

6. **Transparency and constitution** over oral directives and backroom decisions.
   *We codify the rules of the game in written form, accessible to all, so they serve as a safeguard against arbitrariness and chaos, though live communication remains the primary management tool.*

7. **Mental health** over cognitive overload.
   *We consider burnout and chronic overload a critical management failure, not a norm of work. We ruthlessly eliminate legacy tasks and processes to free up resources for creating new value.*

8. **Conflict of opinions** over comfortable consensus.
   *We consciously encourage dissent that challenges the consensus, because truth is born in debate, while comfort is an illusion leading to stagnation.*

9. **Antifragility** over fragile efficiency.
   *We build systems that, under external stress, do not break or merely survive, but become stronger, thanks to the flexibility of their rules and principles.*

10. **Open politics** over informal power.
    *We legitimize and bring into public discourse any lobbying and use of informal influence to subject it to critique. Otherwise, shadow politics becomes a cancer that eats the organization from within.*

## 15 Principles — The Rules of Our Daily Practice

1. Our ultimate goal is to build a self-sustaining system that delivers results consistently, even in our absence.
2. We go to the *gemba* not to find someone to blame, but to understand and improve the process.
3. We are the guarantors of the constitution. Our role is to protect the rules of the game, ensure their fairness, and enforce compliance by all, including ourselves.
4. We create an environment where any problem can be voiced without fear. A hidden problem causes more damage than an uncomfortable conversation.
5. We pay for contribution and results, not for time spent at work. Pay equity is our key priority.
6. We pay for both results and the courage to stop a broken process.
7. We invest in developing "capable minds," not exploiting "warm bodies." Our task is to make every employee an engineer of their own work.
8. We build concrete growth paths, not teach "generally useful" things.
9. A real team is not when everyone does each other's work, but when everyone does their own work so well that it enables others to do theirs easily.
10. We don't wait or catch up. We set the quality and efficiency standards within our industry ourselves.
11. People spend most of their lives at work. Therefore, we build a place of strength, respect, and enjoyment of a job well done.
12. We treat backroom decisions as a symptom of a decaying system.
13. Any crisis is a stress test for the rules. If a rule must be broken in a crisis, change the rule.
14. We protect the team's focus: any new implementation must replace something old.
15. We continuously refine our management architecture, using both proven practices and bold experiments, always aligning them with our values.

---

We call on leaders and managers to embrace this evolutionary paradigm. To leave the era of overseers in the past and enter the era of engineers of human management systems. Our tool is not an order, but a constitution. Our goal is not control, but order born from freedom and meaning. Our strength is not in lone heroes, but in a system that replicates heroism as a standard practice.

This manifesto is a living document. It is not dogma, but a guide to conscious action. It is for those ready to take responsibility for creating order out of chaos.
